### Question 1

A firm faces a downward-sloping demand curve and sells a differentiated product with many close substitutes and low barriers to entry. This market is best characterised as:

A. Perfect competition  
B. Monopolistic competition  
C. Oligopoly  
D. Monopoly  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Product differentiation with many sellers and easy entry defines monopolistic competition. Long-run economic profit is competed away to zero.

*Reading: Economics — The Firm and Market Structures, LOS 1.b*

</details>

### Question 2

The inventory–sales ratio rises sharply. This most likely indicates the economy is:

A. Entering an expansion  
B. Entering a contraction  
C. At a cyclical trough  
D. Unaffected — the ratio is not cyclical  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Sales fall before firms cut production, so unsold inventory builds up. A rising inventory–sales ratio is a classic early-contraction signal.

*Reading: Economics — Understanding Business Cycles, LOS 2.b*

</details>

### Question 3

The spot USD/EUR rate is 1.1000. The one-year US interest rate is 5% and the euro rate is 3%. The one-year forward USD/EUR rate implied by covered interest rate parity is closest to:

> S = 1.1000 · i_USD = 5% · i_EUR = 3%

A. 1.0787  
B. 1.1000  
C. 1.1214  
D. 1.1550  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** F = S × (1 + i_USD)/(1 + i_EUR) = 1.1000 × 1.05/1.03 = 1.1214. The higher-rate currency trades at a forward discount.

*Reading: Economics — Capital Flows and the FX Market, LOS 7.c*

</details>

### Question 4

Which of the following is most accurately described as a lagging economic indicator?

A. The slope of the yield curve  
B. New orders for capital goods  
C. The average duration of unemployment  
D. Building permits issued  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** The average duration of unemployment continues rising after a recovery begins, making it a lagging indicator. The other three are leading.

*Reading: Economics — Understanding Business Cycles, LOS 2.d*

</details>

### Question 5

A central bank raises its policy rate to combat inflation. Compared with fiscal policy, monetary policy most likely has:

A. A longer enactment lag but a shorter impact lag  
B. A shorter enactment lag but a longer impact lag  
C. Shorter lags on both dimensions  
D. Longer lags on both dimensions  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Monetary policy can be enacted quickly by committee decision, but works indirectly through rates, credit and expectations, so its impact lag is long and variable.

*Reading: Economics — Monetary Policy, LOS 4.f*

</details>

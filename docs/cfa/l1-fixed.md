### Question 1

A bond with a modified duration of 7.2 is held when yields rise by 40 basis points. Using duration alone, the estimated percentage price change is closest to:

> ModDur = 7.2 · Δy = +0.40%

A. −2.88%  
B. −0.29%  
C. +2.88%  
D. −7.20%  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** −ModDur × Δy = −7.2 × 0.0040 = −2.88%. The sign is negative because yields rose; convexity would make the true loss slightly smaller.

*Reading: Fixed Income — Yield-Based Bond Duration Measures, LOS 11.c*

</details>

### Question 2

A bond pays a 6% annual coupon while the market yield on comparable bonds is 4.5%. The bond most likely trades at:

A. A discount to par  
B. Par value  
C. A premium to par  
D. A price that cannot be determined  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** When the coupon rate exceeds the required yield, the cash flows are richer than the market demands, so the price is bid above par.

*Reading: Fixed Income — Bond Valuation: Prices and Yields, LOS 6.b*

</details>

### Question 3

Compared with a duration-only estimate, adding a positive convexity adjustment for a large yield increase will:

A. Lower the estimated price  
B. Raise the estimated price  
C. Leave the estimate unchanged  
D. Reverse the sign of the estimate  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Positive convexity means the true price–yield curve lies above the tangent line, so the convexity term is additive for moves in either direction.

*Reading: Fixed Income — Yield-Based Bond Convexity, LOS 12.b*

</details>

### Question 4

Spread duration measures the sensitivity of a corporate bond’s price to a change in:

A. The benchmark government yield  
B. The credit spread only  
C. Both the benchmark and the spread  
D. The realised default frequency  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Spread duration isolates the credit spread component, holding the benchmark curve fixed. Effective duration captures the benchmark move.

*Reading: Fixed Income — Credit Risk, LOS 14.b*

</details>

### Question 5

Which statement about Macaulay and modified duration is correct?

A. Modified duration always exceeds Macaulay duration  
B. They are always equal  
C. Macaulay duration divided by (1 + periodic yield) gives modified duration  
D. Macaulay duration is measured in percent  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** ModDur = MacDur / (1 + periodic yield), so modified duration is always slightly smaller. Macaulay duration is expressed in years.

*Reading: Fixed Income — Yield-Based Bond Duration Measures, LOS 11.a*

</details>

### Question 1

A company reports net income of $480,000, depreciation of $95,000, a $40,000 increase in accounts receivable and a $25,000 increase in accounts payable. Cash flow from operations is closest to:

> NI 480,000 · Dep 95,000 · ΔAR +40,000 · ΔAP +25,000

A. $510,000  
B. $560,000  
C. $600,000  
D. $640,000  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** 480,000 + 95,000 − 40,000 + 25,000 = $560,000. An increase in a receivable uses cash; an increase in a payable provides it.

*Reading: FSA — Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows I, LOS 4.c*

</details>

### Question 2

During a period of rising prices with stable inventory quantities, a firm using LIFO rather than FIFO will report:

A. Higher ending inventory and higher net income  
B. Lower ending inventory and lower net income  
C. Higher ending inventory and lower net income  
D. Lower ending inventory and higher net income  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** LIFO charges the most recent, highest costs to COGS, lowering net income, and leaves older, cheaper costs in ending inventory.

*Reading: FSA — Analysis of Inventories, LOS 6.b*

</details>

### Question 3

Under IFRS, which classification of interest paid on the statement of cash flows is permitted?

A. Operating only  
B. Financing only  
C. Either operating or financing  
D. Investing only  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** IFRS allows interest paid to be classified as either operating or financing. US GAAP requires it to be classified as operating.

*Reading: FSA — Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows I, LOS 4.a*

</details>

### Question 4

A firm consistently reports net income well above cash flow from operations over several years. This is best interpreted as a signal of:

A. Strong earnings quality  
B. A high accruals component and potentially lower earnings quality  
C. Aggressive but permissible tax planning  
D. Nothing — the two measures are unrelated  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A persistent gap between net income and operating cash flow indicates a large accruals component, which historically predicts lower earnings persistence.

*Reading: FSA — Financial Reporting Quality, LOS 10.d*

</details>

### Question 5

A company capitalises rather than expenses a cost in the current year. In that year, the firm will report:

A. Lower net income and lower assets  
B. Higher net income and higher assets  
C. Higher net income and lower assets  
D. Lower net income and higher assets  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Capitalising moves the cost to the balance sheet, raising assets and deferring the expense, which raises current-year net income. Later years bear the amortisation.

*Reading: FSA — Analysis of Long-Term Assets, LOS 7.a*

</details>

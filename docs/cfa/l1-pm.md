### Question 1

Two assets have a correlation of exactly +1.0. Combining them in a portfolio will produce:

> ρ₁,₂ = +1.0

A. A reduction in portfolio standard deviation  
B. No diversification benefit  
C. A risk-free portfolio at some weight  
D. A portfolio with zero expected return  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** With perfect positive correlation the portfolio standard deviation is the weighted average of the individual standard deviations — no benefit at all.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Portfolio Risk and Return: Part I, LOS 1.d*

</details>

### Question 2

A stock has a beta of 1.4. The risk-free rate is 3% and the expected market return is 9%. Its required return under the CAPM is:

> β = 1.4 · R_f = 3% · E(R_m) = 9%

A. 9.0%  
B. 11.4%  
C. 12.6%  
D. 15.6%  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** E(R) = 3% + 1.4 × (9% − 3%) = 3% + 8.4% = 11.4%. Note the market risk premium is 6%, not 9%.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Portfolio Risk and Return: Part II, LOS 2.e*

</details>

### Question 3

A security plots above the security market line. It is best described as:

A. Overvalued, with a negative alpha  
B. Undervalued, with a positive alpha  
C. Correctly valued  
D. Undervalued, but only if beta exceeds 1  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Plotting above the SML means the expected return exceeds what the CAPM requires for its beta — a positive alpha, so it is undervalued.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Portfolio Risk and Return: Part II, LOS 2.f*

</details>

### Question 4

A client has substantial wealth and a long horizon but becomes visibly anxious during drawdowns. The adviser should most appropriately:

A. Follow the higher ability to take risk  
B. Follow the lower willingness to take risk and educate the client  
C. Average the two assessments  
D. Decline the engagement  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** When willingness and ability conflict, the lower governs. The adviser should adopt the more conservative position while educating the client.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Basics of Portfolio Planning and Construction, LOS 4.b*

</details>

### Question 5

An investor holds a losing position because selling would confirm the loss. This bias is best classified and remedied as:

A. A cognitive error, to be corrected with information  
B. An emotional bias, to be moderated  
C. A cognitive error, to be moderated  
D. An emotional bias, to be corrected with information  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Loss aversion is an emotional bias arising from feeling rather than faulty reasoning, so it is generally moderated and accommodated rather than corrected.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — The Behavioral Biases of Individuals, LOS 5.b*

</details>

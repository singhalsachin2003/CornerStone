### Question 1

A firm repurchases shares at a market price above book value per share. The effect on book value per share is:

A. An increase  
B. A decrease  
C. No change  
D. An increase only if funded with cash  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Repurchasing above book value removes more equity per share than the shares carried, so book value per share falls. Below book value it rises.

*Reading: Corporate Issuers — Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases, LOS 1.d*

</details>

### Question 2

A firm earns EPS of $4.00, targets a 40% payout ratio and currently pays $1.00. With an adjustment factor of 0.25, the target payout adjustment model predicts a dividend increase of:

> EPS $4.00 · target payout 40% · D₀ $1.00 · adj 0.25

A. $0.15  
B. $0.25  
C. $0.60  
D. $1.60  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** Expected increase = (4.00 × 0.40 − 1.00) × 0.25 = (1.60 − 1.00) × 0.25 = $0.15. The full gap is closed only gradually.

*Reading: Corporate Issuers — Analysis of Dividends and Share Repurchases, LOS 1.f*

</details>

### Question 3

When estimating the beta of a private firm using the pure-play method, the analyst should relever the unlevered beta using:

A. The comparable company's debt-to-equity ratio  
B. The subject company's target debt-to-equity ratio  
C. The industry average debt-to-equity ratio  
D. A zero debt-to-equity ratio  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Unlever with the comparable's capital structure to strip it out, then relever with the subject firm's target structure. Using the comparable's in both steps is the planted error.

*Reading: Corporate Issuers — Cost of Capital: Advanced Topics, LOS 3.c*

</details>

### Question 4

In a share-exchange acquisition rather than a cash acquisition, the risk that expected synergies fail to materialise is:

A. Borne entirely by the acquirer  
B. Shared between acquirer and target shareholders  
C. Borne entirely by the target  
D. Eliminated by the exchange ratio  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Target shareholders become owners of the combined entity, so they share in any shortfall. In a cash deal the acquirer bears the full risk.

*Reading: Corporate Issuers — Corporate Restructuring, LOS 4.d*

</details>

### Question 5

Which ESG integration approach explicitly excludes entire industries from the investable universe?

A. Best-in-class  
B. Negative screening  
C. Thematic investing  
D. Engagement and active ownership  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Negative screening removes sectors or activities outright. Best-in-class instead selects leaders within every sector.

*Reading: Corporate Issuers — ESG Considerations in Investment Analysis, LOS 2.b*

</details>

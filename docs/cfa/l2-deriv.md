# Derivatives

## Sample questions

These 5 questions are free in the app too. Try each one before opening the answer.

### Question 1

Three months after inception, the forward price on a contract has risen from $50 to $53. With six months remaining and a risk-free rate of 4%, the value to the long is closest to:

> F₀ = $50 · Fₜ = $53 · T − t = 0.5 · r = 4%

A. $2.94  
B. $3.00  
C. $3.06  
D. $0.00  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** V = (Fₜ − F₀)/(1 + r)^(T−t) = 3/(1.04)^0.5 = 3/1.0198 = $2.94. The value is the discounted difference between forward prices.

*Reading: Derivatives — Pricing and Valuation of Forward Commitments, LOS 1.c*

</details>

### Question 2

In the Black–Scholes–Merton model, N(d₂) is best interpreted as:

A. The delta of the call  
B. The risk-neutral probability the option finishes in the money  
C. The actual probability the option finishes in the money  
D. The present value factor applied to the strike  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** N(d₂) is the risk-neutral probability of exercise. N(d₁) is the option's delta, and the actual probability is not used in the model at all.

*Reading: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.d*

</details>

### Question 3

A dealer is short gamma and rebalances a delta hedge only weekly. A large move in the underlying in either direction will most likely produce:

A. A hedging gain  
B. A hedging loss  
C. No effect, because the book is delta neutral  
D. A gain only if the move is upward  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A short gamma position loses on large moves in either direction because the delta hedge is systematically wrong between rebalancing dates.

*Reading: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.h*

</details>

### Question 4

An increase in the volatility of the underlying will, all else equal:

A. Increase call values and decrease put values  
B. Decrease call values and increase put values  
C. Increase both call and put values  
D. Leave both unchanged  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Higher volatility raises the value of optionality on both sides, because the downside is truncated by the option structure while the upside is not.

*Reading: Derivatives — Valuation of Contingent Claims, LOS 2.f*

</details>

### Question 5

Two months after inception of a plain vanilla interest rate swap, market rates have fallen. The value of the swap to the fixed-rate payer is:

A. Positive  
B. Negative  
C. Zero  
D. Positive only if the swap is collateralised  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The fixed payer is locked into paying an above-market rate while receiving less floating. The swap has negative value to that side.

*Reading: Derivatives — Pricing and Valuation of Forward Commitments, LOS 1.f*

</details>

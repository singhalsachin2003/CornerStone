# Economics

## Sample questions

These 5 questions are free in the app too. Try each one before opening the answer.

### Question 1

An investor borrows in a low-interest-rate currency and invests in a high-interest-rate currency. This carry trade is profitable only if:

A. Uncovered interest rate parity holds  
B. Uncovered interest rate parity fails  
C. Covered interest rate parity fails  
D. Purchasing power parity holds in the short run  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** If uncovered parity held, the high-rate currency would depreciate by exactly the interest differential and the trade would earn nothing. Its empirical failure is the source of carry returns.

*Reading: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.d*

</details>

### Question 2

Under the neoclassical growth model, a permanent increase in the savings rate will:

A. Raise the long-run growth rate of output per capita  
B. Raise the level of output per capita but not its long-run growth rate  
C. Have no effect on either the level or the growth rate  
D. Lower the steady-state capital-to-labour ratio  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Diminishing returns to capital mean higher saving raises the steady-state level of output per worker but leaves the long-run growth rate set by technology.

*Reading: Economics — Economic Growth, LOS 2.d*

</details>

### Question 3

Relative purchasing power parity predicts that a country with persistently higher inflation than its trading partners will experience:

A. Currency appreciation  
B. Currency depreciation  
C. No change in the nominal exchange rate  
D. A widening real interest differential only  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Higher relative inflation erodes purchasing power, so relative PPP predicts nominal depreciation roughly equal to the inflation differential.

*Reading: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.b*

</details>

### Question 4

A country converges toward the steady state of countries with similar institutions and savings rates, but not toward all countries. This is best described as:

A. Absolute convergence  
B. Conditional convergence  
C. Club convergence  
D. Divergence  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Club convergence describes convergence only within groups sharing institutional characteristics — the word "institutions" is the tell.

*Reading: Economics — Economic Growth, LOS 2.f*

</details>

### Question 5

Under the FX portfolio balance approach, a country running persistent fiscal deficits will most likely see its currency:

A. Appreciate indefinitely as capital flows in  
B. Appreciate in the short run and depreciate in the long run  
C. Depreciate immediately and permanently  
D. Remain unchanged in both horizons  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Higher rates attract capital and support the currency initially, but investors eventually demand compensation for the growing debt burden, causing depreciation.

*Reading: Economics — Currency Exchange Rates, LOS 1.g*

</details>

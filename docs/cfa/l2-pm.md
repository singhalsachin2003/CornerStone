## Sample questions

### Question 1

A manager has an information coefficient of 0.05, 100 independent bets per year and a transfer coefficient of 0.8. The expected information ratio is closest to:

> IC 0.05 · BR 100 · TC 0.8

A. 0.04  
B. 0.40  
C. 0.50  
D. 4.00  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** IR ≈ IC × √BR × TC = 0.05 × 10 × 0.8 = 0.40. Breadth enters as a square root, so doubling bets does not double the ratio.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Analysis of Active Portfolio Management, LOS 2.d*

</details>

### Question 2

A portfolio holds 400 stocks but all positions reflect a single macroeconomic view. Under the fundamental law of active management, breadth is closest to:

A. 400  
B. 20  
C. 1  
D. 400 divided by the transfer coefficient  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Breadth counts independent decisions, not holdings. One macro view driving every position is a breadth of one.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Analysis of Active Portfolio Management, LOS 2.e*

</details>

### Question 3

A portfolio has a one-day 95% VaR of $1.2m. The most accurate interpretation is:

> VaR₉₅,₁d = $1.2m

A. The maximum loss on any single day is $1.2m  
B. On 5% of days the loss is expected to exceed $1.2m  
C. The average daily loss is $1.2m  
D. Losses will never exceed $1.2m in a month  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** VaR is a quantile of the loss distribution. It states the frequency with which the threshold is breached, never the size of the breach.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Measuring and Managing Market Risk, LOS 5.b*

</details>

### Question 4

A backtest uses financial statement data as later restated rather than as originally reported. This introduces:

A. Survivorship bias  
B. Look-ahead bias  
C. Data snooping bias  
D. Selection bias  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Restated data was not available at the decision date, so the backtest uses information the manager could not have had — look-ahead bias.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Backtesting and Simulation, LOS 6.c*

</details>

### Question 5

An ETF trades persistently at a premium to net asset value. The most likely cause is:

A. A failure of the creation/redemption mechanism  
B. High portfolio turnover within the fund  
C. A high expense ratio  
D. A large number of authorised participants  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** Arbitrage by authorised participants normally closes premiums and discounts. A persistent premium implies the mechanism is impaired.

*Reading: Portfolio Management — Exchange-Traded Funds, LOS 3.c*

</details>

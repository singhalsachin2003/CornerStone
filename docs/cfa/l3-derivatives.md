### Question 1

An investor buys stock at $48 and writes a call with a $52 strike for a $2 premium. The maximum gain on the covered call is:

> S₀ = $48 · X = $52 · premium $2

A. $2  
B. $4  
C. $6  
D. Unlimited  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Maximum gain = (X − S₀) + premium = (52 − 48) + 2 = $6, achieved at or above the strike. Upside beyond $52 is surrendered.

*Reading: Derivatives and Risk Management — Options Strategies*

</details>

### Question 2

A zero-cost collar on a long equity position produces:

A. Unlimited upside with limited downside  
B. Bounded upside and bounded downside  
C. Unlimited downside with limited upside  
D. The same payoff as the underlying  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The long put floors the loss and the short call caps the gain. The premium received funds the premium paid, hence zero cost.

*Reading: Derivatives and Risk Management — Options Strategies*

</details>

### Question 3

A manager holds a $50m portfolio with a beta of 1.1 and wants a beta of 0.7. Futures with a beta of 0.95 have a contract value of $250,000. The required position is closest to:

> V_P $50m · β_P 1.1 · β_T 0.7 · β_f 0.95 · P_f $250,000

A. Buy 84 contracts  
B. Sell 84 contracts  
C. Sell 21 contracts  
D. Buy 21 contracts  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** N = [(0.7 − 1.1)/0.95] × (50,000,000/250,000) = −0.4211 × 200 = −84.2. The negative sign means selling roughly 84 contracts.

*Reading: Derivatives and Risk Management — Swaps, Forwards and Futures Strategies*

</details>

### Question 4

Which condition most strongly argues for leaving foreign currency exposure unhedged?

A. A short investment horizon  
B. High correlation between the currency and the asset return  
C. A long horizon and low correlation between currency and asset returns  
D. High hedging cost combined with a short horizon  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Over long horizons currency effects tend to mean-revert, and low correlation means the currency contributes diversification rather than risk.

*Reading: Derivatives and Risk Management — Currency Management*

</details>

### Question 5

A domestic investor earns 8% on a foreign asset while the foreign currency appreciates 3%. The domestic-currency return is closest to:

> R_FC = 8% · R_FX = 3%

A. 5.00%  
B. 11.00%  
C. 11.24%  
D. 24.00%  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** (1.08)(1.03) − 1 = 1.1124 − 1 = 11.24%. Adding the two returns gives 11.00% and omits the cross-product term.

*Reading: Derivatives and Risk Management — Currency Management*

</details>

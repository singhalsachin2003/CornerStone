### Question 1

A manager expects the yield curve to steepen with short rates unchanged. The most appropriate position is:

A. Extend duration across the curve  
B. Buy the short end and sell the long end  
C. Sell the short end and buy the long end  
D. A duration-neutral butterfly  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Steepening means long yields rise relative to short yields, so long bonds lose. Overweight the short end and underweight the long end.

*Reading: Portfolio Management Pathway — Yield Curve Strategies*

</details>

### Question 2

Relative to a duration-matched bullet, a barbell portfolio will outperform when the yield curve:

A. Shifts in a parallel fashion  
B. Flattens  
C. Steepens  
D. Remains unchanged  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A barbell has greater convexity and benefits from a flattening, since the long-end holding gains more than the short-end holding loses.

*Reading: Portfolio Management Pathway — Yield Curve Strategies*

</details>

### Question 3

A credit portfolio has spread duration of 4.5 and spreads widen by 30 basis points over a year in which spread carry is 2.0%. Ignoring defaults, excess return is closest to:

> SD 4.5 · Δs +30bp · carry 2.0%

A. −1.35%  
B. +0.65%  
C. +2.00%  
D. +3.35%  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** EXR ≈ carry − (SD × Δs) = 2.00% − (4.5 × 0.30%) = 2.00% − 1.35% = +0.65%.

*Reading: Portfolio Management Pathway — Fixed-Income Active Management: Credit Strategies*

</details>

### Question 4

Which execution benchmark is most vulnerable to manipulation by the trader being measured?

A. Arrival price  
B. VWAP computed over the trader's own participation window  
C. Previous close  
D. Implementation shortfall  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A trader who supplies a large share of volume in the measurement window influences the VWAP itself, weakening the benchmark.

*Reading: Portfolio Management Pathway — Trade Strategy and Execution*

</details>

### Question 5

Compared with a fundamental concentrated strategy, a quantitative multifactor equity strategy typically exhibits:

A. Higher active share and higher idiosyncratic risk  
B. Lower active share and risk sourced primarily from factor tilts  
C. Identical active share and active risk  
D. Lower active risk and higher stock-specific risk  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Quantitative strategies hold many names with small deviations, giving lower active share, with risk dominated by systematic factor exposures.

*Reading: Portfolio Management Pathway — Active Equity Investing: Portfolio Construction*

</details>

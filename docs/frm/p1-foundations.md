### Question 1

A portfolio has a return of 12%, a standard deviation of 18% and a beta of 1.2. The risk-free rate is 3%. Its Treynor ratio is closest to:

> R_p 12% · σ 18% · β 1.2 · R_f 3%

A. 0.050  
B. 0.075  
C. 0.090  
D. 0.500  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Treynor = (R_p − R_f)/β = (12% − 3%)/1.2 = 0.075. The Sharpe ratio, using σ, would be 0.50.

*Reading: Foundations of Risk Management — Risk-Adjusted Performance*

</details>

### Question 2

The collapse of Barings Bank is most directly attributed to:

A. An unhedged commodity basis exposure  
B. A single trader controlling both trading and settlement functions  
C. A liquidity mismatch in the repo book  
D. A model error in option pricing  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The failure was one of governance: Nick Leeson controlled both the front and back office, so losses could be concealed. The trade itself was secondary.

*Reading: Foundations of Risk Management — Financial Disasters*

</details>

### Question 3

Two portfolios show identical Sharpe ratios but different Treynor ratios. This most likely indicates that:

A. One portfolio holds significant undiversified specific risk  
B. One portfolio is leveraged  
C. The risk-free rate was measured differently  
D. Both portfolios are fully diversified  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** For fully diversified portfolios the two measures rank identically. Divergence implies specific risk in one portfolio that beta does not capture.

*Reading: Foundations of Risk Management — Risk-Adjusted Performance*

</details>

### Question 4

Under an enterprise risk management framework, the primary responsibility for setting risk appetite lies with:

A. The chief risk officer  
B. The board of directors  
C. Internal audit  
D. The trading desk heads  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The board sets risk appetite; management translates it into limits and the risk function monitors adherence. Audit provides independent assurance.

*Reading: Foundations of Risk Management — Risk Governance*

</details>

### Question 5

A risk that can be transferred at a known cost, such as a hedgeable currency exposure, is best managed by:

A. Holding economic capital against it  
B. Hedging it  
C. Scenario analysis only  
D. Accepting it as an unknown unknown  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Known and hedgeable risks are hedged. Capital covers quantifiable but unhedged risk; scenario analysis addresses the unknown unknowns.

*Reading: Foundations of Risk Management — Risk Taxonomy*

</details>

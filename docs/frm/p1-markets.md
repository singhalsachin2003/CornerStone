# Financial Markets & Products

*FRM® Part I, published weight 30%.* Institutions, market structure, futures, swaps, options, rates, FX, MBS.

## What the outline covers

- Financial institutions and their risks: banks, insurers, pension funds, mutual and hedge funds
- Economic vs regulatory capital; insurance company ratios
- Exchange-traded and OTC market structure, central counterparties
- Forwards and futures: mechanics, margin, hedge ratios, basis risk
- Swaps: cash flows and valuation
- Options: mechanics and payoffs
- Interest rates and interest-rate products
- Foreign exchange risk
- Corporate bonds; mortgages and mortgage-backed securities
- Commodity forwards and futures

### Question 1

The standard deviation of spot price changes is 0.65, the standard deviation of futures price changes is 0.81, and their correlation is 0.80. The minimum variance hedge ratio is closest to:

> σ_S 0.65 · σ_F 0.81 · ρ 0.80

A. 0.64  
B. 0.80  
C. 0.99  
D. 1.25  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** h* = ρ × (σ_S/σ_F) = 0.80 × (0.65/0.81) = 0.80 × 0.8025 = 0.642.

*Reading: Financial Markets and Products — Hedging with Futures*

</details>

### Question 2

Initial margin posted to a central counterparty is intended to cover:

A. Current mark-to-market exposure  
B. Potential future exposure over the close-out period  
C. The clearing member's default fund contribution  
D. Accrued interest on the position  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Initial margin covers potential future exposure between default and close-out. Variation margin settles current mark-to-market.

*Reading: Financial Markets and Products — Central Counterparties*

</details>

### Question 3

An investor buys a call with a strike of $40 for a premium of $3. The breakeven price at expiry is:

> X = $40 · premium $3

A. $37  
B. $40  
C. $43  
D. $46  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**C.** Breakeven = strike + premium = 40 + 3 = $43. Below that the option either expires worthless or recovers less than the premium paid.

*Reading: Financial Markets and Products — Options Mechanics and Payoffs*

</details>

### Question 4

A hedger uses a futures contract on a related but different asset. The residual exposure is best described as:

A. Counterparty risk  
B. Basis risk  
C. Liquidity risk  
D. Operational risk  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Cross-hedging leaves basis risk — the risk that the spot and futures prices do not move together.

*Reading: Financial Markets and Products — Forwards and Futures*

</details>

### Question 5

On a reset date, the value of the floating leg of a plain vanilla interest rate swap is:

A. Zero  
B. Par  
C. The notional plus accrued interest for the full remaining term  
D. Indeterminate without the forward curve  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A floating-rate note reprices to par on each reset date. That single fact collapses most swap valuation questions.

*Reading: Financial Markets and Products — Swaps*

</details>

### Question 1

Under the Merton model, an increase in the volatility of the firm’s assets will:

A. Increase equity value and decrease default probability  
B. Increase equity value and increase default probability  
C. Decrease equity value and increase default probability  
D. Leave equity value unchanged  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Equity is a call on the firm's assets, so higher asset volatility raises its value. It simultaneously raises the probability the assets fall below the debt.

*Reading: Credit Risk — Structural Default Models*

</details>

### Question 2

Holding individual default probabilities constant, an increase in default correlation across a credit portfolio will:

A. Increase expected loss and increase credit VaR  
B. Leave expected loss unchanged and increase credit VaR  
C. Increase expected loss and leave credit VaR unchanged  
D. Reduce both measures  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Expected loss depends only on individual PDs. Correlation fattens the tail of the loss distribution, which is what credit VaR measures.

*Reading: Credit Risk — Portfolio Credit Risk*

</details>

### Question 3

A bank buys credit protection on a sovereign from a bank domiciled in that same sovereign. This is a clear example of:

A. Right-way risk  
B. Wrong-way risk  
C. Basis risk  
D. Settlement risk  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The protection seller is most likely to default precisely when the protection is needed, so exposure and counterparty credit quality move adversely together.

*Reading: Credit Risk — Counterparty Risk and CVA*

</details>

### Question 4

In a CDO structure, an increase in default correlation across the collateral pool will most likely:

A. Benefit the equity tranche and harm the senior tranche  
B. Harm the equity tranche and benefit the senior tranche  
C. Benefit both tranches  
D. Have no effect on either tranche  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** Higher correlation raises the probability of zero losses (helping equity) and of very large losses (harming senior). Senior tranches are effectively short correlation.

*Reading: Credit Risk — Structured Credit and CDOs*

</details>

### Question 5

Which mechanism most directly reduces counterparty credit exposure without changing the underlying trades?

A. Increasing the notional  
B. Close-out netting and collateral agreements  
C. Extending the maturity  
D. Widening the credit spread charged  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Netting offsets positive and negative exposures to the same counterparty, and collateral covers the residual. Neither alters the trades themselves.

*Reading: Credit Risk — Netting, Collateral and Margining*

</details>

The Current Issues topic differs from every other FRM topic area because it:

A. Is not examined  
B. Is replaced substantially each exam cycle  
C. Contains only quantitative material  
D. Carries the largest weight in Part II

### Question 2

The principal supervisory concern with machine learning models used in credit decisions is:

A. Their computational cost  
B. Limited explainability and the resulting model governance gap  
C. Their inability to handle large datasets  
D. That they cannot be backtested  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Supervisors expect firms to explain credit decisions. Opaque models create a governance problem: who validates a model nobody fully understands.

*Reading: Current Issues — AI and Machine Learning in Finance*

</details>

### Question 3

A scenario describing rapid, well-signalled decarbonisation policy is best characterised as:

A. High physical risk and low transition risk  
B. Low physical risk and high transition risk  
C. High physical and high transition risk  
D. Low physical and low transition risk  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Fast policy action raises transition risk for exposed sectors while limiting the warming that drives physical risk. The scenarios deliberately trade the two off.

*Reading: Current Issues — Climate and Transition Risk*

</details>

### Question 4

The 2023 US regional banking stress is best explained by the interaction of:

A. Credit losses on commercial loans and inadequate provisions  
B. Unrealised duration losses and concentrated, digitally mobile uninsured deposits  
C. Operational failures in payment systems  
D. Counterparty defaults in the derivatives market  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Held-to-maturity portfolios carried large unrealised losses while a concentrated uninsured deposit base withdrew faster than liquidity rules assumed.

*Reading: Current Issues — Post-2023 Banking Stress*

</details>

### Question 5

A key financial stability concern with fiat-referenced stablecoins is that they:

A. Cannot be transferred across borders  
B. Are subject to run dynamics if reserve quality or redemption is doubted  
C. Pay a variable interest rate to holders  
D. Are always fully insured by deposit schemes  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A stablecoin is a redeemable claim on a reserve pool. Doubt about reserve quality or redemption capacity produces classic run dynamics.

*Reading: Current Issues — Digital Assets and Stablecoins*

</details>

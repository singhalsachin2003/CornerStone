# Liquidity & Treasury Risk

*FRM® Part II, published weight 15%.* Funding vs market liquidity, LVaR, transfer pricing, IRRBB, LCR and NSFR.

## What the outline covers

- Liquidity risk: funding vs market liquidity
- Liquidity-adjusted VaR and transaction cost measurement
- Cash flow modelling and liquidity stress testing
- Intraday, collateral and contingent liquidity management
- Deposit and wholesale funding structures; transfer pricing
- Balance sheet management; repo markets
- Interest rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB)
- LCR, NSFR and the Basel liquidity framework
- Contingency funding plans

### Question 1

A position worth $50m has a VaR of $2m and a bid–ask spread of 0.40%. Liquidity-adjusted VaR using the exogenous spread approach is closest to:

> V $50m · VaR $2m · spread 0.40%

A. $2.00m  
B. $2.10m  
C. $2.20m  
D. $4.00m  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** LVaR = VaR + ½ × spread × V = 2m + 0.5 × 0.004 × 50m = 2m + 0.1m = $2.10m.

*Reading: Liquidity Risk — Liquidity-Adjusted VaR*

</details>

### Question 2

The liquidity coverage ratio requires a bank to hold high-quality liquid assets sufficient to cover net cash outflows over:

A. 5 days  
B. 30 days  
C. 90 days  
D. One year  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The LCR is a 30-day stressed horizon. The NSFR is the one-year structural measure.

*Reading: Liquidity Risk — Basel Liquidity Framework*

</details>

### Question 3

A fund forced to sell assets to meet redemptions depresses prices, triggering further redemptions and further sales. This is best described as:

A. Basis risk  
B. A liquidity spiral  
C. Wrong-way risk  
D. Regulatory arbitrage  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Funding pressure forces sales, which worsens market liquidity, which worsens funding pressure — the self-reinforcing liquidity spiral.

*Reading: Liquidity Risk — Funding versus Market Liquidity*

</details>

### Question 4

Without funds transfer pricing, a bank’s long-dated lending business will most likely appear:

A. Less profitable than it is, because liquidity cost is over-allocated  
B. More profitable than it is, because liquidity cost is not charged to it  
C. Correctly priced, since liquidity cost is a central function  
D. Unaffected, since FTP is an accounting convention only  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** FTP charges business lines for the liquidity they consume. Without it, long-dated lending appears artificially profitable at the centre's expense.

*Reading: Liquidity Risk — Transfer Pricing*

</details>

### Question 5

Which asset receives no haircut in the LCR high-quality liquid asset calculation?

A. Level 1 assets  
B. Level 2A assets  
C. Level 2B assets  
D. All HQLA receive the same haircut  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**A.** Level 1 assets — cash, central bank reserves and qualifying sovereign debt — are counted at full value. Level 2A and 2B take haircuts and are capped.

*Reading: Liquidity Risk — Basel Liquidity Framework*

</details>

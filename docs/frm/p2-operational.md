### Question 1

In a loss distribution approach, operational loss frequency is most commonly modelled with:

A. A lognormal distribution  
B. A Poisson distribution  
C. A generalised Pareto distribution  
D. A Student t distribution  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Frequency is a count over a fixed period, which the Poisson distribution describes. Severity is typically lognormal, with a Pareto tail.

</details>

### Question 2

A firm’s risk management function both designs the control framework and performs the independent assurance review of it. This most directly compromises:

A. The first line of defence  
B. The separation of the second and third lines of defence  
C. The board's risk appetite statement  
D. The RCSA process only  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Independent assurance belongs to internal audit, the third line. A function cannot independently assure the controls it designed.

*Reading: Operational Risk — Three Lines of Defence*

</details>

### Question 3

A model validated successfully on historical data fails badly in a new market regime. This is best classified as a failure of:

A. Implementation accuracy  
B. Conceptual soundness  
C. Data quality controls  
D. Change management  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** The model was built and coded correctly but rests on assumptions that do not hold in the new regime — a conceptual soundness failure.

*Reading: Operational Risk — Model Risk Management*

</details>

### Question 4

A firm outsources a critical process to a third party that suffers an outage. Regulatory accountability for the disruption rests with:

A. The third party alone  
B. The firm, which cannot outsource accountability  
C. Both parties equally by default  
D. Neither, if the contract contains a force majeure clause  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** Outsourcing an activity never outsources the accountability. The firm remains responsible for the resilience of its critical services.

*Reading: Operational Risk — Third-Party and Outsourcing Risk*

</details>

### Question 5

Internal loss data alone is generally insufficient for operational risk capital because it:

A. Is not audited  
B. Is too sparse in the tail to estimate extreme severity  
C. Overstates high-frequency low-severity events  
D. Cannot be used under Basel rules  

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

**B.** A single firm rarely experiences enough extreme events to estimate the tail, so external data and scenario analysis supplement internal data.

*Reading: Operational Risk — Loss Data Collection*

</details>

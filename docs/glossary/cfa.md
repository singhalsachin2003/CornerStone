# CFA® glossary

226 terms, each written to be read cold. The same glossary is free in the app, with search.

### Accrual accounting {#accrual-accounting}

Recognising revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, regardless of when cash moves.

**Exam angle:** The gap between accrual earnings and cash flow is where most accounting-quality questions live. Persistently high accruals are a warning sign.

### Active share {#active-share}

The proportion of a portfolio’s holdings that differs from its benchmark, from 0% for an index fund to 100% for no overlap.

**Exam angle:** Measures how different a portfolio is; tracking error measures how differently it behaves. A high active share with low tracking error means many small off-benchmark bets that cancel.

### Adverse selection {#adverse-selection}

The problem that arises when the party with better information self-selects into a transaction, leaving the counterparty with the worse risks.

**Exam angle:** Distinct from moral hazard: adverse selection happens before the contract, moral hazard after it.

### Agency problem {#agency-problem}

The conflict that arises when managers act in their own interest rather than that of the owners who employ them.

**Exam angle:** Also runs between shareholders and bondholders — asset substitution after a leveraged deal is the classic case.

### Alpha {#alpha}

Return in excess of what the asset’s risk exposures would predict.

**Exam angle:** Alpha is defined relative to a model. Change the benchmark or add a factor and alpha frequently disappears, which is the whole argument about active management.

### Annuity {#annuity}

A series of equal cash flows at equal intervals. An ordinary annuity pays at the end of each period; an annuity due pays at the beginning.

**Exam angle:** An annuity due is worth exactly (1 + r) times the ordinary annuity of the same flows — one extra period of compounding on every payment.

### Arbitrage pricing theory {#arbitrage-pricing-theory}

*Also: APT*

A model deriving expected returns from exposures to multiple systematic factors, assuming no arbitrage rather than a single market portfolio.

**Exam angle:** More general than CAPM but silent about what the factors are. That silence is both its strength and its practical weakness.

### Arithmetic mean {#mean}

*Also: average*

The sum of the observations divided by their number.

**Exam angle:** The right estimate of a single future period’s return. For growth already achieved over several periods, the geometric mean is the honest number.

### Asset location {#asset-location}

Deciding which account type holds which asset, so that heavily taxed returns sit in tax-sheltered accounts.

**Exam angle:** Distinct from asset allocation, and one of the few reliably free sources of after-tax return.

### Balance sheet {#balance-sheet}

*Also: statement of financial position*

A statement of what a company owns and owes at a single point in time.

```
Assets = Liabilities + Equity
```

### Basis risk {#basis-risk}

The risk that a hedge and the exposure it hedges do not move together, leaving a residual.

**Exam angle:** Arises from mismatched underlying, maturity or location. A hedge is a swap of price risk for basis risk, not an elimination of risk.

### Behavioural bias {#behavioural-bias}

A systematic departure from rational decision-making, either cognitive — an error of reasoning that education can correct — or emotional, which usually has to be accommodated.

**Exam angle:** The cognitive/emotional split determines the adviser’s response, and that distinction is what gets tested rather than the list of bias names.

### Beta {#beta}

The sensitivity of an asset’s return to the market’s return — its share of systematic risk.

```
β = Cov(R_i, R_m) / Var(R_m)
```

**Exam angle:** A beta above one amplifies market moves in both directions. Estimated betas are unstable and are often adjusted toward one.

### Black–Scholes–Merton model {#black-scholes}

*Also: BSM, Black-Scholes*

A closed-form model for European option prices, assuming lognormal prices, constant volatility and continuous frictionless trading.

**Exam angle:** Its assumptions are known to be wrong — constant volatility most obviously, which is why the implied volatility smile exists at all.

### Book value {#book-value}

The accounting value of equity: assets less liabilities as reported.

**Exam angle:** Price-to-book works best where assets are marked near fair value — banks — and worst where value is intangible.

### Bootstrap {#bootstrapping-statistics}

Resampling the observed data with replacement to estimate the sampling distribution of a statistic, without assuming a parametric form.

**Exam angle:** Distinct from bootstrapping a spot curve in fixed income, which is a different operation entirely under the same word.

### Bootstrapping (yield curve) {#bootstrapping-curve}

Deriving spot rates sequentially from the prices of coupon bonds, using each solved rate to strip the next.

**Exam angle:** A different operation from the statistical bootstrap, which resamples data. Same word, unrelated technique.

### Business cycle {#business-cycle}

The recurring pattern of expansion, peak, contraction and trough in economic activity.

**Exam angle:** Sector rotation questions follow directly from cycle position — defensives late, cyclicals early.

### Business risk {#business-risk}

The uncertainty in operating income arising from the nature of the business itself, before any financing decision.

**Exam angle:** Financial risk is what leverage adds on top. Keeping the two separate is the point of the distinction.

### Call option {#call-option}

The right, without the obligation, to buy an asset at the strike price by expiry.

**Exam angle:** The buyer’s loss is capped at the premium; the naked writer’s loss is unbounded. That asymmetry drives most option exam questions.

### Callable bond {#callable-bond}

A bond the issuer may redeem early, usually when rates have fallen.

```
Value of callable = Value of straight bond − Value of call
```

**Exam angle:** The investor is short the option, so a callable bond yields more and its upside is capped near the call price.

### Capital allocation line {#capital-allocation-line}

*Also: CAL*

The set of risk–return combinations available by mixing a risky portfolio with the risk-free asset.

**Exam angle:** Its slope is the Sharpe ratio of the risky portfolio, so the steepest CAL identifies the optimal risky portfolio for everyone.

### Capital asset pricing model {#capm}

*Also: CAPM*

A model stating that an asset’s expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the equity risk premium.

```
E(R) = R_f + β × [E(R_m) − R_f]
```

**Exam angle:** Prices systematic risk only. Its empirical record is poor, which is why multifactor models exist — but it remains the exam’s default.

### Capital budgeting {#capital-budgeting}

The process of deciding which long-term investments a company should make.

**Exam angle:** Use incremental after-tax cash flows. Sunk costs are excluded; opportunity costs and externalities are included.

### Capital call {#capital-call}

*Also: drawdown*

The general partner’s demand that limited partners transfer part of their committed capital.

**Exam angle:** Commitments are not investments. The gap between committed and drawn capital is the origin of the denominator problem and of the J-curve.

### Capital structure {#capital-structure}

The mix of debt and equity a company uses to fund itself.

**Exam angle:** Modigliani–Miller says the mix is irrelevant without taxes, distress costs or information asymmetry. Everything interesting comes from relaxing those assumptions.

### Capitalisation rate {#cap-rate}

*Also: cap rate*

Net operating income divided by property value — a property’s unlevered income yield.

```
Value = NOI / cap rate
```

**Exam angle:** Functions like the inverse of a P/E ratio. A falling cap rate means prices rising faster than income.

### Carried interest {#carried-interest}

*Also: carry*

The general partner’s share of a private fund’s profits, typically around 20%.

### Carry trade {#carry-trade}

Borrowing in a low interest rate currency to invest in a high interest rate one, profiting if the exchange rate does not move against you.

**Exam angle:** Returns are negatively skewed: long stretches of small gains punctuated by sharp unwinds. A textbook case of a strategy whose Sharpe ratio flatters it.

### Cash conversion cycle {#cash-conversion-cycle}

The time between paying for inventory and collecting cash from the customer.

```
CCC = Days inventory + Days receivable − Days payable
```

**Exam angle:** A negative cycle means suppliers fund the business. Retailers and marketplaces often run one.

### Cash flow from operations {#cfo}

*Also: CFO, operating cash flow*

Cash generated by the core business, before investment and financing.

**Exam angle:** The indirect method starts at net income and adds back non-cash charges and working capital movements. Classification of interest paid differs between IFRS and US GAAP.

### Central limit theorem {#central-limit-theorem}

*Also: CLT*

For a large enough sample, the distribution of the sample mean approaches normal regardless of the shape of the underlying population.

**Exam angle:** Thirty observations is the conventional threshold. It justifies using normal-based confidence intervals on means — not on individual observations.

### Code of Ethics {#code-of-ethics}

The aspirational statement of how members and candidates should conduct themselves. It sets the principles; the Standards of Professional Conduct are what is actually enforced.

### Coefficient of determination {#r-squared}

*Also: R-squared, R2*

The fraction of the dependent variable’s variation explained by the regression.

**Exam angle:** Never falls when a variable is added, however useless that variable is. Adjusted R² penalises extra regressors and is the honest comparison between models of different size.

### Collar {#collar}

Holding the underlying, buying a put and writing a call, bounding the outcome between a floor and a cap.

**Exam angle:** A zero-cost collar chooses strikes so the premiums offset. Nothing is free — the cost is the upside given away.

### Common share {#common-share}

*Also: ordinary share, common stock*

An ownership claim on a company’s residual cash flows and assets, normally carrying a vote and ranking last in liquidation.

### Composite {#composite}

All portfolios managed to the same strategy or objective, grouped together for GIPS reporting.

**Exam angle:** Every fee-paying discretionary portfolio must be in at least one composite. That requirement is what stops a firm from showing only its winners.

### Confidence interval {#confidence-interval}

A range that will contain the true parameter in a stated proportion of repeated samples.

**Exam angle:** It is a statement about the procedure, not about this one interval. "95% probability the mean is in this range" is the classic misreading.

### Contango and backwardation {#contango-backwardation}

Contango is a futures price above the spot price; backwardation is a futures price below it.

**Exam angle:** A long roll in contango loses as the contract converges to spot. This is why commodity index returns diverge so far from spot commodity prices.

### Continuous compounding {#continuous-compounding}

Compounding over infinitely small intervals. The limit of periodic compounding as the number of periods per year goes to infinity.

```
FV = PV × e^(r × t)
```

**Exam angle:** The default convention in derivatives pricing and in most FRM formulas, which is why the same position can look differently priced in two parts of the curriculum.

### Convexity {#convexity}

The curvature of the price–yield relationship — how duration itself changes as yields move.

**Exam angle:** Positive convexity is good for the holder: prices rise more than duration predicts and fall less. Callable bonds and MBS exhibit negative convexity as rates fall.

### Core inflation {#core-inflation}

Inflation excluding food and energy prices, which are volatile and often driven by supply rather than demand.

**Exam angle:** Central banks watch core for the signal and headline for the mandate.

### Corporate governance {#corporate-governance}

The system of controls by which a company is directed and held accountable to its stakeholders.

### Correlation {#correlation}

*Also: correlation coefficient*

Covariance divided by the product of the two standard deviations, giving a unit-free number between −1 and +1.

```
ρ = Cov(X, Y) / (σₓ × σᵧ)
```

**Exam angle:** Measures linear association only. A perfect non-linear relationship can show near-zero correlation, and correlation tends to rise sharply in a crisis — exactly when diversification was being relied on.

### Cost of equity {#cost-of-equity}

The return equity holders require for bearing the residual risk of the business.

**Exam angle:** Usually estimated with CAPM, sometimes with a dividend discount rearrangement or a build-up model for private firms. All three are estimates, and they rarely agree.

### Coupon rate {#coupon-rate}

The annual interest a bond pays, as a percentage of par.

**Exam angle:** Fixed for the life of a plain vanilla bond. A floater resets against a reference rate plus a quoted margin.

### Covariance {#covariance}

A measure of how two variables move together. Positive means they tend to move the same way; its size depends on the units of both variables.

**Exam angle:** Unbounded and therefore hard to interpret on its own. Correlation is covariance scaled to [−1, 1], which is why correlation is what gets quoted.

### Covered call {#covered-call}

Holding the underlying and writing a call against it, collecting the premium in exchange for capping the upside.

**Exam angle:** Not a hedge. It gives up the right tail to earn income and leaves the downside almost fully intact.

### Credit default swap {#credit-default-swap}

*Also: CDS*

A contract in which the buyer pays a periodic premium and receives compensation if a specified borrower suffers a credit event.

**Exam angle:** Buying protection is economically like shorting the credit. The spread is a market-implied view of default probability and recovery together, not of default alone.

### Credit spread {#credit-spread}

The extra yield a borrower pays over a risk-free benchmark of the same maturity, compensating for default and liquidity risk.

**Exam angle:** Widening spreads hurt even when the issuer never defaults, and spread duration measures that exposure separately from rate duration.

### Currency hedging {#currency-hedging}

Using forwards, futures or options to reduce a portfolio’s exposure to exchange rate movements on foreign assets.

**Exam angle:** The hedge ratio is a deliberate choice, not a binary. Hedging cost is driven by the interest rate differential, not by any view on the currency.

### Current ratio {#current-ratio}

Current assets divided by current liabilities, a rough test of short-term solvency.

**Exam angle:** The quick ratio strips out inventory, which is the least liquid current asset and the one most likely to be stale.

### Current yield {#current-yield}

A bond’s annual coupon divided by its current market price — the cash income it throws off this year per unit invested.

**Exam angle:** Ignores any pull to par, so it overstates return on a premium bond and understates it on a discount bond.

### Data mining {#data-mining}

*Also: data snooping*

Searching a dataset repeatedly until a relationship appears, then presenting it as though it had been hypothesised in advance.

**Exam angle:** Test on out-of-sample data. A relationship with no economic rationale that only shows up after many passes is the definition of the problem.

### Deferred tax {#deferred-tax}

Tax arising from timing differences between accounting profit and taxable profit, recognised as an asset or a liability.

**Exam angle:** A deferred tax liability that never reverses behaves like equity. Analysts often reclassify it for that reason.

### Delta {#delta}

The change in an option’s value for a small change in the underlying price.

**Exam angle:** Ranges 0 to 1 for a call and −1 to 0 for a put. Roughly the hedge ratio, and loosely read as the risk-neutral probability of finishing in the money.

### Delta hedging {#delta-hedging}

Offsetting an option position with the underlying so the combined position is insensitive to small price moves.

**Exam angle:** Only holds locally and only for an instant. Gamma is precisely the reason it has to be rebalanced, and rebalancing is what costs money.

### Depreciation {#depreciation}

The systematic allocation of a tangible asset’s cost over its useful life.

**Exam angle:** Accelerated methods shift expense forward, lowering early income and raising later income. Useful life and salvage value are management estimates and are a standard quality-of-earnings lever.

### Derivative {#derivative}

A contract whose value depends on the price of something else — an asset, a rate, an index or an event.

### Derivatives overlay {#overlay}

Adjusting a portfolio’s exposures with derivatives while leaving the underlying holdings untouched.

**Exam angle:** Cheaper and faster than trading the physicals, and it keeps the manager’s selection intact. It introduces basis risk and a margin obligation.

### Diligence and reasonable basis {#diligence-and-reasonable-basis}

The requirement that any recommendation or action rest on adequate research and analysis that could be defended if challenged.

**Exam angle:** Relying on a third-party model is acceptable only after making reasonable enquiries into its soundness. Delegation does not transfer the duty.

### Distribution waterfall {#waterfall}

The agreed order in which a private fund’s proceeds are shared between limited and general partners.

**Exam angle:** European waterfalls return all capital before any carry is paid; American ones pay deal by deal. The difference is worth a great deal to the GP and is what clawbacks correct.

### Diversification {#diversification}

Combining imperfectly correlated assets so that portfolio risk falls below the weighted average of the individual risks.

**Exam angle:** The benefit comes from correlation below one, not from the number of holdings. Twenty correlated positions diversify less than five uncorrelated ones.

### Dividend discount model {#dividend-discount-model}

*Also: DDM, Gordon growth model*

Valuing a share as the present value of its expected future dividends.

```
V₀ = D₁ / (r − g)
```

**Exam angle:** Requires r > g and a dividend that actually grows at a constant rate. Extremely sensitive to the denominator — a half-point change in g can move the value by a third.

### Dividend policy {#dividend-policy}

How a company decides to return cash to shareholders, and in what form.

**Exam angle:** A cut is read as a signal about future earnings, which is why firms smooth dividends and prefer buybacks for variable returns of capital.

### DuPont analysis {#dupont-analysis}

Decomposing ROE into its drivers — profitability, efficiency and leverage — to identify what is actually moving it.

### Duration {#duration}

The sensitivity of a bond’s price to a change in yield, and equivalently the weighted average time to receiving its cash flows.

**Exam angle:** Macaulay duration is in years; modified duration is the percentage price change per one percent yield move. Effective duration is the one to use when cash flows are not fixed.

### DV01 {#dv01}

*Also: PV01, basis point value, BPV*

The change in a position’s value for a one basis point change in yield, expressed in currency.

**Exam angle:** The practitioner’s hedging unit: hedge ratios are DV01 of the exposure divided by DV01 of the hedge. FRM uses it far more than duration.

### Earnings per share {#earnings-per-share}

*Also: EPS*

Net income available to common shareholders, divided by the weighted average shares outstanding.

**Exam angle:** Diluted EPS assumes every in-the-money convertible, option and warrant converts. It is the conservative figure and the one that gets compared.

### Earnings quality {#earnings-quality}

The degree to which reported earnings are sustainable and backed by cash rather than by estimates and one-offs.

**Exam angle:** Standard flags: earnings persistently above cash flow, rising days sales outstanding, and repeated "non-recurring" charges.

### EBITDA {#ebitda}

Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a rough proxy for operating cash generation.

**Exam angle:** Ignores capital intensity and working capital entirely. Two firms with identical EBITDA and very different capex are not comparable.

### Effective annual rate {#effective-annual-rate}

*Also: EAR, annual equivalent rate*

The rate that, compounded once a year, gives the same growth as a stated nominal rate compounded more often.

```
EAR = (1 + r_nominal / m)^m − 1
```

**Exam angle:** EAR rises with compounding frequency and converges on e^r − 1 under continuous compounding. Comparing two quoted rates of different frequencies without converting to EAR is a trap.

### Effective duration {#effective-duration}

Duration measured by repricing the bond under small parallel shifts in the benchmark curve, so that changes in cash flows are captured.

**Exam angle:** The only valid duration for callable, putable and mortgage-backed bonds, where the cash flows themselves depend on rates.

### Efficient frontier {#efficient-frontier}

The set of portfolios offering the highest expected return for each level of risk.

**Exam angle:** Estimated from expected returns, variances and covariances — all of which are noisy. Small input changes produce wildly different "optimal" portfolios.

### Enterprise value {#enterprise-value}

*Also: EV*

The value of the whole business regardless of how it is financed: market capitalisation plus debt and minorities, less cash.

```
EV = Market cap + Debt + Minority interest − Cash
```

**Exam angle:** Pair EV with pre-interest metrics such as EBITDA or EBIT; pair market cap with post-interest metrics such as net income.

### Factor investing {#factor-investing}

*Also: smart beta*

Building portfolios around systematic return drivers — value, size, momentum, quality, low volatility — rather than around individual security selection.

**Exam angle:** Factor premia are long-horizon and endure long stretches of underperformance. A factor that never hurts is usually a factor that has been fitted.

### Fair dealing {#fair-dealing}

Treating all clients fairly when disseminating recommendations or taking investment action. Fair does not mean identical.

**Exam angle:** Different service levels are allowed if disclosed and available to all. What is not allowed is giving some clients a head start on a change of recommendation.

### Fiduciary duty {#fiduciary-duty}

The obligation to act in another party’s interest ahead of your own, with the care and loyalty that relationship demands.

**Exam angle:** Identify who the client actually is before applying it. For a pension, the duty runs to the beneficiaries, not to the company that hired you.

### Financial leverage {#financial-leverage}

*Also: gearing*

The use of debt to finance assets, magnifying returns to equity in both directions.

### Fiscal policy {#fiscal-policy}

Government use of spending and taxation to influence aggregate demand.

**Exam angle:** Faster to take effect than monetary policy once enacted, but slower to enact. That asymmetry is the usual discussion point.

### Forward contract {#forward-contract}

A private agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date. Customisable, and settled once at expiry.

**Exam angle:** Carries counterparty risk that builds over the life of the contract, because nothing is exchanged until the end.

### Forward rate {#forward-rate}

The interest rate agreed today for borrowing or lending over a future period, implied by today’s spot curve.

**Exam angle:** Implied forwards are break-even rates, not forecasts. A curve that implies rising rates is telling you what must happen for two strategies to tie, not what the market predicts.

### Free cash flow {#free-cash-flow}

*Also: FCF, FCFF, FCFE*

Cash available after the investment needed to maintain the business. FCFF is available to all capital providers; FCFE is what remains for equity holders after debt service.

```
FCFF = CFO + Interest × (1 − tax rate) − CapEx
```

**Exam angle:** Discount FCFF at WACC, FCFE at the cost of equity. Mixing the two is the most common valuation error in the exam.

### Free float {#free-float}

The portion of a company’s shares actually available to public investors, excluding strategic and locked-up holdings.

**Exam angle:** Index providers weight by free float so the index remains replicable.

### Front-running {#front-running}

Trading ahead of a client order to benefit from the price move that order will cause.

### Fundamental law of active management {#fundamental-law}

Expected active return is a function of skill, the number of independent decisions taken, and how fully those decisions are expressed in the portfolio.

```
IR ≈ IC × √Breadth × Transfer coefficient
```

**Exam angle:** Breadth must be independent bets. Applying the same macro view to two hundred stocks is one decision, not two hundred.

### Future value {#future-value}

*Also: FV*

What a sum invested today grows to by a stated date at a stated rate.

```
FV = PV × (1 + r)^n
```

### Futures contract {#futures-contract}

A standardised, exchange-traded forward, marked to market daily through a clearing house.

**Exam angle:** Daily settlement is the whole difference: it strips out counterparty risk and it makes the cash flow timing different from an otherwise identical forward.

### Gamma {#gamma}

The rate at which delta changes as the underlying moves.

**Exam angle:** Highest at the money and near expiry. High gamma means a delta hedge goes stale quickly and must be rebalanced more often.

### General partner {#general-partner}

*Also: GP*

The manager of a private fund, responsible for investment decisions and bearing unlimited liability for the partnership.

**Exam angle:** Limited partners commit capital and are liable only for their commitment. The split of economics between them is the fund’s central negotiation.

### Geometric mean {#geometric-mean}

The constant rate that would produce the observed compound growth over the whole period.

```
Geometric mean = [(1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)…(1 + rₙ)]^(1/n) − 1
```

**Exam angle:** Always ≤ the arithmetic mean, and strictly less whenever returns vary. The gap widens with volatility — which is why volatile strategies advertise the arithmetic figure.

### GIPS {#gips}

*Also: Global Investment Performance Standards*

Voluntary standards for calculating and presenting investment performance, designed so that a prospective client can compare firms on a like-for-like basis.

**Exam angle:** Compliance is claimed by the firm as a whole — never by a single composite, product or individual.

### Goals-based investing {#goals-based-investing}

Structuring wealth into separate portfolios for separate objectives, each with its own horizon and risk tolerance, rather than one portfolio for one risk number.

**Exam angle:** Mentally sub-optimal by mean-variance standards and behaviourally far more durable, because clients abandon a plan they cannot connect to anything.

### Goodwill {#goodwill}

The excess of a purchase price over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired.

**Exam angle:** Not amortised; tested for impairment. A large impairment is an admission that the acquisition was overpriced.

### Gross domestic product {#gdp}

*Also: GDP*

The market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a period.

**Exam angle:** Final goods only. Counting intermediate output as well is double-counting, which is exactly what the value-added method exists to avoid.

### Harmonic mean {#harmonic-mean}

The reciprocal of the average of reciprocals. Used for averaging ratios over a fixed money amount, such as the cost of shares bought at different prices.

**Exam angle:** Harmonic ≤ geometric ≤ arithmetic, always. Cost averaging is the standard exam application.

### Hedge fund {#hedge-fund}

A privately offered pooled vehicle with broad freedom over strategy, leverage and short selling.

**Exam angle:** A legal structure and fee model, not a strategy. "Hedge fund" says nothing about what the fund actually does.

### Heteroskedasticity {#heteroskedasticity}

Regression errors whose variance is not constant across observations.

**Exam angle:** Coefficients stay unbiased; the standard errors do not. So t-statistics are wrong and inference is unreliable — the fix is robust standard errors, not a different slope.

### High-water mark {#high-water-mark}

The highest value an investor’s holding has previously reached, above which incentive fees are charged.

**Exam angle:** Stops the manager charging twice for recovering the same losses. It does not stop them closing the fund and starting again.

### Human capital {#human-capital}

The present value of an individual’s expected future earnings, treated as an asset alongside their financial wealth.

**Exam angle:** Bond-like for a tenured professional and equity-like for a commission earner. The financial portfolio should lean against it, which is why concentration in an employer’s stock is doubly wrong.

### Hurdle rate {#hurdle-rate}

The minimum return a fund must deliver before the manager earns an incentive fee.

**Exam angle:** A hard hurdle charges only on returns above it; a soft hurdle charges on the whole gain once it is cleared.

### Illiquidity premium {#illiquidity-premium}

The extra expected return demanded for holding an asset that cannot be sold quickly at a fair price.

**Exam angle:** Reported private-market volatility is understated because valuations are smoothed and infrequent, which flatters risk-adjusted measures.

### Immunisation {#immunisation}

Structuring a bond portfolio so that price and reinvestment effects cancel, locking in a return over a horizon.

**Exam angle:** Requires matching duration to the horizon and rebalancing as time passes and yields move. It protects against parallel shifts, not twists.

### Implementation shortfall {#implementation-shortfall}

The difference between the return on a paper portfolio and the return actually achieved, capturing delay, market impact, commissions and unfilled orders.

**Exam angle:** The honest measure of execution cost, because it counts the trades that never happened at the price that was wanted.

### Implied volatility {#implied-volatility}

The volatility that, put into an option pricing model, returns the option’s observed market price.

**Exam angle:** A price quoted in volatility units rather than a forecast. Plotting it against strike gives the smile or skew.

### Income statement {#income-statement}

*Also: profit and loss, P&L*

A statement of revenue, expenses and profit over a period.

### Independence and objectivity {#independence-and-objectivity}

The requirement to use reasonable care and judgement to keep independence, and to refuse any gift or pressure that could compromise it.

**Exam angle:** Modest, customary token gifts from clients may be accepted with disclosure to the employer; anything from a company you cover is the problem case.

### Inflation {#inflation}

A sustained rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money.

**Exam angle:** Distinguish demand-pull from cost-push: the policy response differs, and a central bank tightening into a supply shock is a recognised error.

### Information ratio {#information-ratio}

Active return divided by tracking error — value added per unit of benchmark-relative risk.

**Exam angle:** The natural measure for a benchmarked active manager, where the Sharpe ratio would mostly reflect the benchmark.

### Initial and variation margin {#margin-futures}

Initial margin is the good-faith deposit required to open a futures position; variation margin is the daily cash transfer that settles the day’s gain or loss.

**Exam angle:** Falling below the maintenance margin triggers a call back to the initial level, not to the maintenance level.

### Interest coverage ratio {#interest-coverage}

EBIT divided by interest expense — how many times earnings cover the interest bill.

**Exam angle:** A primary input to credit ratings and to covenant tests.

### Interest rate parity {#interest-rate-parity}

*Also: covered interest rate parity, IRP*

The condition that the forward exchange rate differs from the spot rate by exactly the interest rate differential, so hedged returns are equal across currencies.

**Exam angle:** Covered parity is an arbitrage relationship and holds tightly. Uncovered parity is an expectation and does not — that gap is what the carry trade harvests.

### Internal rate of return {#internal-rate-of-return}

*Also: IRR*

The discount rate at which a project’s NPV is zero.

**Exam angle:** A cash flow stream that changes sign more than once can have more than one IRR, or none. That is the whole reason NPV is the preferred rule.

### Investment policy statement {#ips}

*Also: IPS*

The written agreement setting out an investor’s objectives, constraints and the rules the portfolio will be run by.

**Exam angle:** Objectives are return and risk. Constraints are liquidity, time horizon, tax, legal and regulatory, and unique circumstances — memorise the five.

### J-curve {#j-curve}

The typical shape of a private fund’s returns: negative early, as fees are paid before value is realised, then rising.

### Key rate duration {#key-rate-duration}

*Also: partial duration*

Sensitivity to a change in one segment of the yield curve, holding the other segments fixed.

**Exam angle:** Needed because real curves twist and steepen. A portfolio can be duration-neutral overall and still lose heavily on a curve reshaping.

### Kurtosis {#kurtosis}

*Also: excess kurtosis, fat tails*

The weight of a distribution’s tails. Excess kurtosis is kurtosis minus 3, so a normal distribution has excess kurtosis of zero.

**Exam angle:** Leptokurtic — positive excess kurtosis — means extreme outcomes are likelier than normal. Financial returns are reliably leptokurtic, which is why normal-based VaR understates the tail.

### Leveraged buyout {#leveraged-buyout}

*Also: LBO*

Acquiring a company using a large proportion of borrowed money, secured against the target’s own assets and cash flows.

**Exam angle:** Returns come from deleveraging, operational improvement and multiple expansion. Separating the three is the standard analysis.

### Liability-driven investing {#liability-driven-investing}

*Also: LDI*

Managing a portfolio against the liabilities it must fund rather than against a market index.

**Exam angle:** Risk becomes the mismatch between asset and liability sensitivities, so a "safe" cash portfolio can be highly risky against long-dated liabilities.

### LIFO and FIFO {#lifo-fifo}

Inventory cost-flow assumptions. FIFO expenses the oldest costs first; LIFO expenses the newest.

**Exam angle:** LIFO is permitted under US GAAP and prohibited under IFRS. When prices rise, LIFO gives lower income and lower inventory — the LIFO reserve converts between the two.

### Linear regression {#linear-regression}

*Also: ordinary least squares, OLS*

Fitting a straight line through data by minimising the sum of squared residuals, to explain a dependent variable by one or more independent variables.

### Lock-up period {#lock-up}

The period during which an investor may not redeem from a fund.

**Exam angle:** Gates and side pockets do a similar job in a crisis, and all three are liquidity risk rather than fine print.

### Lognormal distribution {#lognormal-distribution}

The distribution of a variable whose natural logarithm is normally distributed. Bounded below by zero and right-skewed.

**Exam angle:** Used for asset prices because a price cannot go negative, while continuously compounded returns can be modelled as normal. That pairing is the basis of Black–Scholes–Merton.

### Loyalty to employer {#loyalty-to-employer}

The duty to act for the employer’s benefit, not to deprive it of your skills, and not to divulge confidential information.

**Exam angle:** Preparing to leave is allowed; taking client lists, models or records built on the employer’s time is not. Nothing changes once notice is served.

### Margin (equity trading) {#margin-equity}

Borrowing from a broker to buy securities, using the securities as collateral.

**Exam angle:** A margin call comes when equity falls below the maintenance margin. Leverage magnifies both the return and the speed of the call.

### Market capitalisation {#market-capitalisation}

*Also: market cap*

Share price multiplied by shares outstanding — the market value of a company’s equity.

**Exam angle:** Not the value of the firm. Enterprise value adds net debt, and is what you compare against EBITDA or EBIT.

### Market efficiency {#market-efficiency}

*Also: efficient market hypothesis, EMH*

The proposition that prices already reflect available information, so consistently beating the market on that information is not possible.

**Exam angle:** Three forms: weak (past prices), semi-strong (all public information), strong (all information, public and private). Technical analysis fails under weak-form; fundamental analysis fails under semi-strong.

### Market impact {#market-impact}

The adverse price move caused by the act of trading itself.

**Exam angle:** Rises with order size relative to normal volume. Trading more slowly reduces impact and increases the risk of the price moving away meanwhile.

### Market manipulation {#market-manipulation}

Transactions or information intended to distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume, in order to mislead other participants.

**Exam angle:** Split into information-based and transaction-based. Intent is what is examined, and it is usually established by what the person expected to happen.

### Market-capitalisation-weighted index {#cap-weighted-index}

An index in which weights are proportional to constituents’ market values.

**Exam angle:** Self-rebalancing, and the only weighting every investor could hold at once. Its criticism is that it systematically holds more of whatever has already risen.

### Material non-public information {#material-nonpublic-information}

*Also: MNPI, inside information*

Information that has not been released to the market and that a reasonable investor would want before trading. Acting or causing others to act on it is prohibited.

**Exam angle:** Materiality and non-publicity are separate tests and both must hold. Selective disclosure to analysts does not make information public.

### Modified duration {#modified-duration}

The approximate percentage change in a bond’s price for a one percentage point change in yield.

```
Modified duration = Macaulay duration / (1 + y/m)
```

**Exam angle:** A linear approximation. It always overstates the loss from a rise in yields and understates the gain from a fall, because the true relationship is convex.

### Monetary policy {#monetary-policy}

A central bank’s management of money supply and interest rates to pursue price stability and, in some mandates, employment.

**Exam angle:** Works with long and variable lags, which is why policy is set on forecasts rather than on today’s data.

### Money-weighted return {#money-weighted-return}

*Also: MWR, dollar-weighted return*

The IRR of a portfolio including the timing and size of every contribution and withdrawal.

**Exam angle:** Measures the investor’s experience, not the manager’s skill, because the client controls the cash flows. Use it to judge an account, never to compare managers.

### Moneyness {#moneyness}

Whether exercising an option now would pay: in-the-money, at-the-money or out-of-the-money.

**Exam angle:** Intrinsic value is what exercise would yield today; time value is everything else in the premium, and it decays to zero at expiry.

### Monte Carlo simulation {#monte-carlo-simulation}

Estimating a distribution of outcomes by repeatedly drawing random inputs from assumed distributions and recomputing the result.

**Exam angle:** The output is only as good as the assumed input distributions and correlations. It generates precision, not accuracy.

### Moral hazard {#moral-hazard}

The incentive to take more risk once someone else bears the consequences.

**Exam angle:** Central to both economics and risk management: deposit insurance, bailouts and bonus structures are the standard examples.

### Mosaic theory {#mosaic-theory}

Combining public information with non-material non-public information to reach a material conclusion. Doing so is permitted.

**Exam angle:** The defence of a good analyst. Keep the notes: the file showing how the conclusion was assembled is what distinguishes a mosaic from an inside tip.

### Multicollinearity {#multicollinearity}

Two or more independent variables in a regression that are highly correlated.

**Exam angle:** The giveaway is a high R² with individually insignificant t-statistics. The regression as a whole explains plenty; it cannot attribute the explanation.

### Multifactor model {#multifactor-model}

A model explaining returns by exposure to several systematic factors rather than to the market alone.

**Exam angle:** Fama–French adds size and value to the market factor; Carhart adds momentum. Each factor is a claim that a risk is priced, and each is contested.

### Net present value {#net-present-value}

*Also: NPV*

The present value of a project’s cash inflows less the present value of its outflows. A positive NPV adds to shareholder wealth.

**Exam angle:** NPV and IRR agree on accept/reject for a conventional independent project, and can disagree on ranking mutually exclusive ones. Where they disagree, NPV wins.

### Neutral rate {#neutral-rate}

*Also: natural rate of interest, r-star*

The real policy rate that neither stimulates nor restrains the economy when output is at potential.

**Exam angle:** Unobservable and estimated. Most arguments about whether policy is tight are really arguments about where neutral sits.

### Normal distribution {#normal-distribution}

*Also: Gaussian distribution, bell curve*

A symmetric, bell-shaped distribution fully described by its mean and variance.

**Exam angle:** Approximately 68% of outcomes fall within one standard deviation, 95% within two and 99% within 2.58. Those three numbers appear constantly in VaR and confidence-interval questions.

### Notional principal {#notional-principal}

The reference amount used to compute a derivative’s payments, which is usually never exchanged.

**Exam angle:** Notional wildly overstates economic exposure. Comparing a derivatives book’s notional with a balance sheet is a category error.

### Null hypothesis {#null-hypothesis}

*Also: H0*

The statement a test assumes true until the data give sufficient reason to reject it. Always the one containing equality.

**Exam angle:** Failing to reject is not the same as proving. The null survives for want of evidence, not because it was shown to be true.

### Off-balance-sheet financing {#off-balance-sheet}

Arrangements that give a company the use of an asset or the benefit of borrowing without showing the liability on its balance sheet.

**Exam angle:** Largely curtailed for leases by IFRS 16 and ASC 842, which brought most operating leases on balance sheet.

### Operating leverage {#operating-leverage}

The sensitivity of operating income to a change in sales, driven by the proportion of fixed costs.

**Exam angle:** High fixed costs amplify both directions. Pair it with financial leverage and small revenue moves produce very large earnings moves.

### Opportunity cost {#opportunity-cost}

The value of the best alternative given up by choosing one course of action.

### Option-adjusted spread {#oas}

*Also: OAS*

The spread over the benchmark curve once the value of any embedded option has been stripped out, making bonds with and without options comparable.

**Exam angle:** Model-dependent: it moves with the volatility assumption. Two desks can compute different OAS on the same bond and both be right.

### Output gap {#output-gap}

The difference between actual and potential output, as a percentage of potential.

**Exam angle:** A positive gap signals inflationary pressure. Potential output is estimated, so the gap is revised heavily after the fact.

### p-value {#p-value}

The smallest significance level at which the null could be rejected — the probability of a result at least this extreme if the null were true.

**Exam angle:** Not the probability the null is true. Reject when p < α.

### Par value {#par-value}

*Also: face value, principal*

The amount a bond repays at maturity, and the base on which the coupon is calculated.

### Pecking order theory {#pecking-order-theory}

The observation that firms prefer internal funds first, then debt, and issue equity last.

**Exam angle:** Driven by information asymmetry: issuing equity signals that management thinks the shares are expensive, so the announcement itself moves the price.

### Performance attribution {#performance-attribution}

Decomposing a portfolio’s return relative to its benchmark into the decisions that produced it — typically allocation, selection and interaction.

**Exam angle:** Brinson attribution is the standard framework. Returns-based attribution infers exposures from return series; holdings-based uses the actual positions.

### Perpetuity {#perpetuity}

An annuity with no end date — a level cash flow received forever.

```
PV = C / r
```

**Exam angle:** Only valid where r > 0 and the payment is level. A growing perpetuity uses C / (r − g), and requires r > g or the value is meaningless.

### Preference share {#preferred-share}

*Also: preferred stock*

A share with a stated dividend that ranks ahead of common equity but behind all debt, usually without a vote.

**Exam angle:** Economically a hybrid. Cumulative preference shares accrue missed dividends; non-cumulative do not.

### Prepayment risk {#prepayment-risk}

The risk that borrowers repay mortgage principal early, most often when rates fall and refinancing becomes attractive.

**Exam angle:** The cause of negative convexity in MBS: exactly when a normal bond would rally, the cash flows come back to be reinvested at the new lower rate.

### Present value {#present-value}

*Also: PV, discounted value*

What a future cash flow is worth today, found by discounting it at a rate that reflects its risk and timing.

```
PV = FV / (1 + r)^n
```

**Exam angle:** Match the rate to the period. A quarterly cash flow discounted at an annual rate is the single most common arithmetic slip in the exam.

### Price elasticity of demand {#elasticity}

*Also: elasticity*

The percentage change in quantity demanded for a one percent change in price.

**Exam angle:** Demand is elastic when the magnitude exceeds one. Revenue rises with a price cut only where demand is elastic — the standard exam application.

### Price-to-earnings ratio {#price-earnings}

*Also: P/E, PE ratio*

Share price divided by earnings per share.

**Exam angle:** Meaningless when earnings are negative or near zero, and heavily affected by accounting choices. Trailing uses reported earnings; forward uses forecasts.

### Price-weighted index {#price-index}

An index in which each constituent’s weight is proportional to its share price.

**Exam angle:** A high-priced share dominates regardless of company size, and a split changes the weights without changing anything real. The Dow is the famous example.

### Private credit {#private-credit}

*Also: direct lending*

Lending to companies outside the public bond and syndicated loan markets, usually at floating rates with bespoke covenants.

**Exam angle:** Return comes from illiquidity and complexity as much as from credit risk. Marks are infrequent, so reported volatility understates the real thing.

### Private equity {#private-equity}

Equity investment in companies that are not publicly traded, typically through a closed-end fund with a fixed life.

### Protective put {#protective-put}

Holding the underlying and buying a put, which sets a floor under the position at the cost of the premium.

**Exam angle:** Economically insurance. The premium is a certain cost paid to remove an uncertain one, and over time it is a drag.

### Purchasing power parity {#purchasing-power-parity}

*Also: PPP*

The proposition that exchange rates adjust so that identical goods cost the same across countries.

**Exam angle:** A long-run anchor, badly violated in the short run. Relative PPP — that the exchange rate moves with the inflation differential — is the testable version.

### Put option {#put-option}

The right, without the obligation, to sell an asset at the strike price by expiry.

### Put–call parity {#put-call-parity}

The no-arbitrage relationship linking a European call, a European put, the underlying and a risk-free bond of the same strike and expiry.

```
c + PV(X) = p + S₀
```

**Exam angle:** Rearranging it constructs synthetic positions, and a violation is a textbook arbitrage. European options only.

### Putable bond {#putable-bond}

A bond the holder may sell back to the issuer early, usually when rates have risen.

```
Value of putable = Value of straight bond + Value of put
```

### Real versus nominal {#real-vs-nominal}

A nominal figure is measured in current prices; a real figure has been adjusted for inflation and so measures actual volume or purchasing power.

### Rebalancing {#rebalancing}

Trading back to target weights after market moves have shifted them.

**Exam angle:** Mechanically it sells what rose and buys what fell. Calendar and percentage-band rules trade off transaction costs against drift.

### Record retention {#record-retention}

The requirement to keep the records supporting analysis, recommendations and actions. Seven years is the recommended minimum where no regulation says otherwise.

**Exam angle:** Records belong to the employer. They do not travel with you to the next firm.

### Reinvestment risk {#reinvestment-risk}

The risk that coupons and principal must be reinvested at a lower rate than expected.

**Exam angle:** Moves opposite to price risk. Setting a horizon equal to Macaulay duration is what makes the two offset — the basis of immunisation.

### REIT {#reit}

*Also: real estate investment trust*

A listed vehicle holding income-producing property, generally required to distribute most of its income to keep favourable tax treatment.

**Exam angle:** Correlates with equities in the short run and with property in the long run — a recurring point in diversification questions.

### Required rate of return {#required-rate-of-return}

The minimum return an investor demands for holding an asset, given its risk.

### Return on equity {#roe}

*Also: ROE*

Net income as a percentage of average shareholders’ equity.

```
ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Financial leverage
```

**Exam angle:** That three-part split is the DuPont decomposition. A rising ROE driven only by leverage is a different story from one driven by margin.

### Return smoothing {#smoothed-returns}

Understated volatility caused by infrequent or appraisal-based valuations rather than by genuinely stable prices.

**Exam angle:** Inflates Sharpe ratios and depresses measured correlation with public markets. Unsmoothing the series usually removes most of the apparent diversification benefit.

### Rho {#rho}

The sensitivity of an option’s value to a change in the risk-free rate.

### Risk budgeting {#risk-budgeting}

Allocating a portfolio’s total risk across strategies or managers, rather than allocating its capital.

**Exam angle:** Two managers with equal capital rarely consume equal risk. Budgeting by capital and reviewing by risk is how concentration arrives unnoticed.

### Risk tolerance {#risk-tolerance}

How much risk an investor is both able and willing to take. Ability comes from the balance sheet and horizon; willingness comes from temperament.

**Exam angle:** Where the two conflict, the prudent course is the lower of them, plus client education. That is an examinable judgement.

### Roll yield {#commodity-roll-yield}

The return from rolling a futures position forward as contracts expire, positive in backwardation and negative in contango.

### Securitisation {#securitisation}

Pooling loans and issuing securities backed by their cash flows, usually through a bankruptcy-remote special purpose vehicle.

### Serial correlation {#serial-correlation}

*Also: autocorrelation*

Regression errors correlated with their own past values.

**Exam angle:** Common in time series. Positive serial correlation deflates standard errors, which inflates t-statistics and manufactures significance that is not there.

### Share repurchase {#share-buyback}

*Also: buyback*

A company buying its own shares, reducing the share count.

**Exam angle:** Equivalent to a dividend in cash terms but more flexible and often tax-advantaged. Raises EPS mechanically, which is not the same as creating value.

### Sharpe ratio {#sharpe-ratio}

Excess return over the risk-free rate, per unit of total risk.

```
Sharpe = (R_p − R_f) / σ_p
```

**Exam angle:** Uses total risk, so it judges a portfolio held on its own. It flatters strategies with negatively skewed returns, because standard deviation does not see the tail.

### Short selling {#short-selling}

Selling borrowed securities in the expectation of buying them back cheaper.

**Exam angle:** Loss is theoretically unlimited, and the borrow can be recalled at the worst moment. That asymmetry is what makes a short squeeze possible.

### Skewness {#skewness}

The asymmetry of a distribution. Positive skew has a long right tail; negative skew has a long left tail.

**Exam angle:** Returns on short-volatility and credit strategies are negatively skewed: many small gains, occasional large losses. Mean and standard deviation alone hide that.

### Special situations {#special-situations}

Investing around a specific corporate event — restructuring, distress, spin-off or litigation — where the outcome depends more on the event than on the market.

### Spot rate {#spot-rate}

*Also: zero rate, zero-coupon rate*

The yield on a single cash flow received at one future date, with no intervening coupons.

**Exam angle:** Valuing a bond off the spot curve rather than a single YTM is what removes the reinvestment assumption.

### Stakeholder {#stakeholder}

Any party with an interest in the company — shareholders, creditors, employees, customers, suppliers, regulators and the wider community.

### Standard deviation {#standard-deviation}

*Also: volatility, vol, sigma*

The square root of variance, in the same units as the data. In finance it is the usual proxy for total risk.

**Exam angle:** Penalises upside and downside equally, which is its main criticism and the motivation for downside measures like semi-deviation and VaR.

### Standard error {#standard-error}

The standard deviation of a sample statistic, usually of the sample mean.

```
SE = σ / √n
```

**Exam angle:** Falls with the square root of sample size, so quartering the standard error needs sixteen times the data.

### Standards of Professional Conduct {#standards-of-professional-conduct}

The seven enforceable standards covering professionalism, integrity of capital markets, duties to clients, duties to employers, investment analysis and recommendations, conflicts of interest, and responsibilities as a member or candidate.

**Exam angle:** Questions usually turn on which standard applies rather than on whether something felt wrong. Learn the seven headings in order.

### Statement of cash flows {#cash-flow-statement}

A statement reconciling opening and closing cash, split into operating, investing and financing activities.

**Exam angle:** Cash flow is much harder to manipulate than earnings, which is why analysts start here when something looks wrong.

### Straddle {#straddle}

Buying a call and a put at the same strike and expiry — a position on the size of a move rather than its direction.

**Exam angle:** A long straddle needs a move larger than the combined premium. Buying one into an announcement usually means buying already-elevated implied volatility.

### Strategic asset allocation {#strategic-asset-allocation}

*Also: SAA, policy portfolio*

The long-run target mix of asset classes, set from the investor’s objectives and constraints.

**Exam angle:** Explains the large majority of the variation in a portfolio’s returns over time — far more than security selection.

### Style drift {#style-drift}

A manager gradually moving away from the strategy the mandate describes.

**Exam angle:** Often invisible in returns until the market turns, because drifting toward whatever is working flatters performance while it lasts.

### Suitability {#suitability}

The requirement to judge an investment against the client’s objectives, constraints and circumstances before recommending or making it.

**Exam angle:** For a portfolio the test is the effect on the total portfolio, not the merits of the security in isolation.

### Sunk cost {#sunk-cost}

A cost already incurred and unrecoverable, and therefore irrelevant to any decision now.

### Survivorship bias {#survivorship-bias}

Distortion caused by studying only the entities that lasted long enough to appear in the sample.

**Exam angle:** Inflates measured fund returns, because closed and merged funds drop out of the index. A staple of both ethics and quant questions.

### Sustainable growth rate {#sustainable-growth-rate}

The rate at which a firm can grow without changing its capital structure or issuing new equity.

```
g = ROE × retention ratio
```

### Swap {#swap}

An agreement to exchange one stream of cash flows for another over time.

**Exam angle:** A plain vanilla interest rate swap is a series of forward rate agreements, and can also be read as being long one bond and short another.

### Systematic risk {#systematic-risk}

*Also: market risk, non-diversifiable risk*

Risk affecting the whole market, which cannot be diversified away.

**Exam angle:** The only risk investors are compensated for. That claim is the foundation of CAPM.

### Tactical asset allocation {#tactical-asset-allocation}

*Also: TAA*

Short-term deliberate deviation from the strategic allocation to exploit a perceived opportunity.

### Tax-loss harvesting {#tax-loss-harvesting}

Realising losses deliberately to offset gains elsewhere, while keeping the portfolio’s economic exposure broadly intact.

**Exam angle:** Defers tax rather than avoiding it, because the replacement asset carries a lower basis. Wash-sale rules restrict repurchasing the same security.

### Theta {#theta}

The change in an option’s value from the passage of one day, all else equal.

**Exam angle:** Negative for a long option: time decay works against the buyer and accelerates into expiry.

### Time value of money {#time-value-of-money}

*Also: TVM*

The principle that a sum available today is worth more than the same sum later, because today’s sum can be invested. Every discounting and compounding formula in the curriculum is an application of it.

### Time-weighted return {#time-weighted-return}

*Also: TWR*

The compound growth of one unit of currency in a portfolio, with the effect of external cash flows removed by chain-linking sub-period returns.

**Exam angle:** The GIPS-required basis for comparing managers, precisely because it is immune to client cash flow timing.

### Tracking error {#tracking-error}

*Also: active risk*

The standard deviation of the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns.

**Exam angle:** A mandate constraint as much as a statistic. A tight tracking error budget limits how much a manager can differ from the index at all.

### Tranche {#tranche}

One slice of a securitisation with a defined priority of payment, so that losses hit the junior slices before the senior ones.

**Exam angle:** Subordination is the credit enhancement. Correlation between the underlying loans is what determines whether the senior tranche is really safe.

### Treynor ratio {#treynor-ratio}

Excess return per unit of systematic risk, using beta in the denominator.

**Exam angle:** The right measure when the portfolio is one component of a diversified whole, where only its beta matters.

### Two and twenty {#two-and-twenty}

*Also: management and incentive fee*

The classic alternative fee model: an annual management fee of around 2% of assets plus around 20% of profits.

**Exam angle:** Compounding fees matter enormously over a fund life. Whether the incentive fee is computed before or after the management fee changes the answer.

### Type I error {#type-i-error}

*Also: false positive, alpha error*

Rejecting a true null hypothesis. Its probability is the significance level, α.

**Exam angle:** Type I and Type II trade off against each other at a fixed sample size. Only more data improves both.

### Type II error {#type-ii-error}

*Also: false negative, beta error*

Failing to reject a false null hypothesis. One minus its probability is the power of the test.

### Unsystematic risk {#unsystematic-risk}

*Also: idiosyncratic risk, specific risk*

Risk specific to one company or sector, which diversification can remove.

**Exam angle:** Not rewarded, because it can be eliminated for free. Bearing it is a choice, not a risk premium.

### Value at Risk {#value-at-risk}

*Also: VaR*

The loss that will not be exceeded over a stated horizon with a stated confidence — for example, a 1-day 99% VaR of £1m means losses should exceed £1m on about one day in a hundred.

**Exam angle:** Says nothing about how bad the breach is when it happens, and is not subadditive, so the VaR of a combined book can exceed the sum of its parts. Both failings motivate expected shortfall.

### Variance {#variance}

The average squared deviation from the mean — the standard measure of dispersion.

**Exam angle:** A sample variance divides by n − 1, not n. Dividing by n gives a biased estimate, and the correction is examined directly.

### Vega {#vega}

The change in an option’s value for a one point change in implied volatility.

**Exam angle:** Always positive for a plain long option, call or put. Long options are long volatility.

### Venture capital {#venture-capital}

Equity investment in early-stage companies with high failure rates and highly skewed outcomes.

**Exam angle:** Returns are driven by a handful of holdings. Median fund performance is a poor guide to what a top-quartile fund does.

### Vintage year {#vintage-year}

The year a private fund makes its first investment, and the basis on which its performance is compared with peers.

**Exam angle:** Comparing across vintages compares market conditions, not skill. Entry valuations dominate private returns.

### Wealth transfer {#wealth-transfer}

Moving assets to the next generation or to charity, structured for tax efficiency and for the family’s intentions.

**Exam angle:** Lifetime gifting transfers future growth out of the estate. The trade-off is control, and it is usually the real obstacle rather than the tax.

### Weighted average cost of capital {#wacc}

*Also: WACC*

The blended required return on a company’s capital, weighting each source by its share of the total at market value.

```
WACC = w_d × r_d × (1 − t) + w_e × r_e
```

**Exam angle:** Use market weights and target weights, not book values. The tax shield applies to debt only, which is why debt looks cheap before considering the distress it brings.

### Working capital {#working-capital}

Current assets less current liabilities — the short-term capital tied up in operations.

**Exam angle:** Growing revenue with faster-growing receivables means the growth is being funded by the balance sheet, not the customer.

### Working capital management {#working-capital-management}

Managing receivables, payables, inventory and cash so the business has liquidity without tying up more capital than it needs.

### Yield curve {#yield-curve-econ}

The relationship between yield and maturity for bonds of the same credit quality.

**Exam angle:** Inversion — short rates above long — has preceded most recent recessions and is treated as a leading indicator rather than a cause.

### Yield curve strategy {#yield-curve-strategy}

Positioning a bond portfolio for an expected change in the shape of the curve — bullet, barbell or ladder structures expressing different views.

**Exam angle:** A barbell outperforms a duration-matched bullet when the curve flattens, and has more convexity. Duration alone cannot express a curve view.

### Yield to maturity {#yield-to-maturity}

*Also: YTM, redemption yield*

The single discount rate that equates a bond’s price to the present value of its cash flows.

**Exam angle:** Assumes every coupon is reinvested at the YTM and the bond is held to maturity. Both assumptions usually fail, which is why realised return differs from YTM.
